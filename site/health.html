Health and home

What you live in is what you breathe.

The science on indoor air is public and consistent. Here is what the major agencies say, and where the materials in a typical house come into it.

90%of time spent indoorsAmericans spend about 90 percent of their time inside.U.S. EPA
2-5xindoor versus outdoorIndoor levels of some pollutants are often 2 to 5 times outdoor levels.U.S. EPA
47%of U.S. homeshave dampness or mold, averaged across seven studies.Berkeley Lab
21%of current U.S. asthmais attributable to dampness and mold in homes. That is 4.6 million cases.Berkeley Lab
10-50%of indoor environmentsin North America, Europe, Australia, India, and Japan are affected by dampness.World Health Organization
$3.5Ba yearis the estimated U.S. cost of asthma tied to dampness and mold.Berkeley Lab

"It is not the mold itself. It is the mycotoxins. They are invisible, they get in the air, and in a sealed home they build up like radon."

The World Health Organization found sufficient evidence linking indoor dampness to:

  • Respiratory symptoms
  • Respiratory infections
  • Worsening of asthma

Mycotoxins are toxic compounds some molds produce. Researchers have not conclusively identified which agents cause which health effects, and WHO sets no numeric safe threshold. Its recommendation is to prevent persistent dampness and choose materials that resist moisture and mold. Source

Dr. Ritchie Shoemaker's clinical research estimates that nearly 1 in 4 people carry genes that make these toxins harder to clear. This is one researcher's estimate and not a consensus figure. Source

It is not only mold.

The EPA lists these as sources of formaldehyde in homes:

  • Medium density fiberboard, the highest emitter
  • Particleboard in subfloors, shelving, and cabinets
  • Hardwood plywood paneling
  • Other pressed wood products made with urea-formaldehyde resin

The EPA associates formaldehyde exposure with burning eyes and throat, wheezing and coughing, skin rash, and asthma attacks at high levels, and says it may cause cancer in humans. Source

If your doctor says change your environment

That instruction is where our family started. The hard part is that the environment they describe is not for sale. You have to build it.

  • Take out carpets and porous materials
  • Leave behind belongings from the affected house
  • Rebuild with materials that do not feed mold or off-gas
